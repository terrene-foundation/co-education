# Chicago/Turabian Citation Guide

The Chicago Manual of Style (CMOS) is widely used in history, the social sciences, and many academic journals. Turabian is a student-oriented subset of Chicago style. Chicago offers two citation systems: Notes-Bibliography (NB) and Author-Date (AD). The social sciences typically use Author-Date; humanities use Notes-Bibliography.

## Why Chicago/Turabian Matters

Several leading journals (_American Journal of Sociology_, _Journal of Modern History_, _American Economic Review_) use Chicago style. If your research uses historical or archival sources, or if your department specifies Chicago, you need to know both systems.

## System 1: Notes-Bibliography (NB)

Used in humanities. Citations appear as footnotes or endnotes, with a full bibliography at the end.

### Footnote Format

First citation: full details. Subsequent citations: shortened form.

**First footnote**:

> 1. John Hattie and Helen Timperley, "The Power of Feedback," _Review of Educational Research_ 77, no. 1 (2007): 81-112, https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487.

**Shortened footnote** (subsequent citations):

> 5. Hattie and Timperley, "The Power of Feedback," 86.

**Bibliography entry** (note the differences from the footnote):

> Hattie, John, and Helen Timperley. "The Power of Feedback." _Review of Educational Research_ 77, no. 1 (2007): 81-112. https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487.

### Key NB Source Types

**Book**:

Footnote:

> 1. John Biggs and Catherine Tang, _Teaching for Quality Learning at University_, 4th ed. (Maidenhead: Open University Press, 2011), 97.

Bibliography:

> Biggs, John, and Catherine Tang. _Teaching for Quality Learning at University_. 4th ed. Maidenhead: Open University Press, 2011.

**Edited volume chapter**:

Footnote:

> 2. Graham Nuthall, "The Hidden Lives of Learners," in _International Handbook of Educational Research_, vol. 1, ed. Mary James and Alex Pollard (London: Routledge, 2011), 143.

Bibliography:

> Nuthall, Graham. "The Hidden Lives of Learners." In _International Handbook of Educational Research_, vol. 1, edited by Mary James and Alex Pollard, 139-158. London: Routledge, 2011.

## System 2: Author-Date (AD)

Used in sciences and social sciences. More similar to APA.

### In-Text Citations

**One author**: (Hattie 2009) or Hattie (2009)

**Two authors**: (Brown and Lee 2014) -- note: "and" not "&"

**Three or more authors**: (Brown et al. 2021)

**Page reference**: (Hattie 2009, 83)

**Multiple works**: (Hattie 2009; Biggs 2011)

### Reference List Format

The reference list is titled "References" (not "Bibliography" -- that term is reserved for the NB system).

**Journal article**:

> Hattie, John, and Helen Timperley. 2007. "The Power of Feedback." _Review of Educational Research_ 77 (1): 81-112. https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487.

**Book**:

> Biggs, John, and Catherine Tang. 2011. _Teaching for Quality Learning at University_. 4th ed. Maidenhead: Open University Press.

**Working paper**:

> Mollick, Ethan, and Lilach Mollick. 2023. "Using AI to Implement Effective Teaching Strategies in Classrooms." EdWorkingPaper 23-1. Providence, RI: Annenberg Institute at Brown University. https://doi.org/10.26300/example.

**Institutional report**:

> United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization. 2024. _Global Education Monitoring Report: Technology in Education_. Paris: UNESCO.

**Newspaper article**:

> Carey, Benedict. 2014. "Studying for Exams? Forget What You Know About Good Study Habits." _New York Times_, September 4, 2014.

**Dataset**:

> OECD. 2024. "Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) 2022 Results." OECD iLibrary. Accessed March 1, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1787/example.

**Website**:

> American Psychological Association. 2023. "How to Cite Generative AI in APA Style." Last modified September 5, 2023. https://apastyle.apa.org/blog/how-to-cite-chatgpt.

## Key Differences: Chicago AD vs. APA 7th

| Feature                     | Chicago AD           | APA 7th                             |
| --------------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| In-text connector           | "and"                | "&"                                 |
| Year placement in reference | After author name    | After author name (in parentheses)  |
| Article title               | In quotation marks   | No quotation marks, sentence case   |
| Journal volume/issue        | 77 (1): 81-112       | _77_(1), 81-112                     |
| Publisher location          | Included             | Not included (APA 7th dropped this) |
| "et al." threshold          | 4+ authors           | 3+ authors                          |
| DOI format                  | https://doi.org/xxx. | https://doi.org/xxx                 |

### Worked Example: Same Source in Both Styles

**Chicago AD**:

> Hattie, John, and Helen Timperley. 2007. "The Power of Feedback." _Review of Educational Research_ 77 (1): 81-112. https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487.

**APA 7th**:

> Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. (2007). The power of feedback. _Review of Educational Research_, _77_(1), 81-112. https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487

**Key differences in this example**: Chicago uses full first names, "and" not "&", year after author, article title in quotes, no italics on volume, and a period after the DOI.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Mixing NB and AD systems**: Choose one system and use it consistently throughout. Never use footnotes for some citations and parenthetical for others.

2. **Using "&" in Chicago style**: Chicago always uses "and" between author names, both in-text and in the reference list. The ampersand is an APA convention.

3. **Omitting publisher location in Chicago**: Unlike APA 7th (which dropped locations), Chicago still requires city of publication for books.

4. **Incorrect capitalization of article titles**: In Chicago AD, article titles are in headline case _and_ quotation marks. In APA, titles are in sentence case with no quotation marks.

5. **Forgetting the period after DOIs**: Chicago style places a period after the DOI link. APA does not.

6. **Using "Bibliography" with Author-Date**: The NB system uses "Bibliography"; the AD system uses "References." Using the wrong heading signals unfamiliarity with the style.
